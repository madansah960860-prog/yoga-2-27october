Pranayama & meditation

The quiet half of yoga

Long before yoga was associated with flexible poses, it was a practice of breath and attention. These techniques need no equipment and only a few minutes, and they are the part of yoga you can take anywhere.

Why the breath?

Prana, breath and attention

In Sanskrit, prana means breath or life force, and ayama means extension or control. Pranayama is the practice of guiding the breath deliberately. It is the fourth of the eight limbs of yoga described in the Yoga Sutras, and it is traditionally taught as a bridge between the body and the mind.

You don’t need any philosophy to feel the effect of breathing well. Breathing is one of the few body functions that happens automatically yet can also be controlled at will. Many people notice that slowing the breath, especially lengthening the exhale, helps them feel calmer and more settled. Scientists continue to study how slow breathing relates to the nervous system and stress.

Before you start

  • Sit comfortably with a long spine, on a cushion or chair, or lie down.
  • Breathe through the nose unless a technique says otherwise.
  • Practice on an empty or light stomach.
  • Never strain. If you feel dizzy, anxious or short of breath, return to normal breathing.

Techniques

Eight breathing practices

Listed roughly from gentlest to most demanding. Learn them in order, and spend at least a week with each before moving on.

1

Three-Part Breath

Dirga Pranayama
Beginner3–5 minutesSeated or lying

Three-Part Breath teaches you to fill the lungs fully, from the belly to the ribs to the upper chest, then empty them in reverse. It is the foundation for every other technique.

  1. Lie down or sit comfortably, with one hand on the belly and one on the chest.
  2. Inhale through the nose into the belly, then the ribs, then the upper chest.
  3. Exhale slowly from the chest, then the ribs, then the belly.
  4. Continue for 10–15 rounds without forcing the breath.
2

Victorious Breath

Ujjayi Pranayama
BeginnerDuring practiceSeated or moving

Ujjayi creates a soft, ocean-like sound by gently narrowing the back of the throat. It is used throughout Ashtanga and Vinyasa classes to keep the breath slow and steady during movement.

  1. Breathe in through the nose.
  2. Exhale through an open mouth as if fogging a mirror, noticing the gentle constriction in the throat.
  3. Keep that slight constriction, close the mouth and breathe through the nose.
  4. The sound should be soft enough that only you, or someone right beside you, can hear it.
3

Alternate-Nostril Breathing

Nadi Shodhana
Beginner5 minutesSeated

A balancing practice in which the breath alternates between the left and right nostrils. Its name means “channel cleaning.” Many practitioners use it before meditation.

  1. Sit tall. Rest the left hand in the lap. Fold the index and middle fingers of the right hand toward the palm.
  2. Close the right nostril with the thumb and inhale through the left.
  3. Close the left nostril with the ring finger, release the thumb and exhale through the right.
  4. Inhale through the right, close it, and exhale through the left. That is one round.
  5. Continue for 5–10 rounds, keeping the breath smooth and unhurried.

Skip it if you have a blocked nose or a cold; breathe normally instead.

4

Equal or Box Breathing

Sama Vritti
Beginner3–5 minutesAnywhere

Sama vritti means “equal fluctuation.” Inhale and exhale for the same count. Adding a short, gentle pause after each creates a four-sided “box.”

  1. Inhale through the nose for a count of four.
  2. Pause for four, without straining or gripping the throat.
  3. Exhale for four.
  4. Pause for four. Repeat for several minutes.

Leave out the pauses if holding the breath feels uncomfortable, or if you are pregnant or have high blood pressure.

5

Extended Exhale

Vishama Vritti
Beginner3–10 minutesLying or seated

Making the exhale longer than the inhale is one of the simplest ways to slow down. Many people use it in the evening or before sleep.

  1. Inhale through the nose for a count of four.
  2. Exhale slowly through the nose for a count of six.
  3. Once that feels easy, try exhaling for eight.
  4. Keep the breath quiet and relaxed; never gasp on the inhale.
6

Humming Bee Breath

Bhramari Pranayama
Beginner–Intermediate2–5 minutesSeated

Named after the black bee, bhramari, this technique uses a gentle hum on the exhale. The vibration and sound give the mind something simple to rest on.

  1. Sit tall and close the eyes. Optionally, rest the index fingers on the cartilage at the front of the ears.
  2. Inhale through the nose.
  3. Exhale slowly while making a steady, low humming sound with the lips closed.
  4. Repeat five to ten times, then sit quietly and notice the after-effect.
7

Cooling Breath

Sitali & Sitkari
Beginner–Intermediate1–3 minutesSeated

In Sitali, you inhale through a curled tongue; in Sitkari, you inhale through the teeth. In both, the inhaled air feels cool. They are traditionally practiced in hot weather.

  1. Curl the sides of the tongue into a tube and extend it slightly past the lips. If you can’t curl your tongue, part the lips and inhale through gently closed teeth.
  2. Inhale slowly through the tongue or teeth, feeling the cool air.
  3. Close the mouth and exhale through the nose.
  4. Repeat for eight to ten breaths.

Avoid in cold air or if you have a respiratory condition aggravated by cool, dry air.

8

Skull-Shining Breath

Kapalabhati
Intermediate1–2 minutesSeated

Kapalabhati uses short, sharp exhales driven by the belly, with passive inhales. It is an energizing practice, and it is the most demanding on this list.

  1. Sit tall with the hands on the knees.
  2. Take a full breath in, then exhale fully.
  3. Inhale halfway, then snap the lower belly in to push air out through the nose; let the inhale happen on its own.
  4. Begin with 15–20 slow pumps, then rest with several normal breaths. Build gradually.

Not suitable for everyone: avoid Kapalabhati during pregnancy or menstruation, and if you have high blood pressure, heart disease, epilepsy, a hernia, recent abdominal surgery, glaucoma, or panic or anxiety disorders. Stop immediately if you feel light-headed. Learning it with a teacher is recommended.

Meditation

Five ways to sit with your mind

Meditation isn’t about emptying the mind. It is about noticing where your attention goes, and kindly bringing it back. Try each method for a few days and keep the ones that suit you.

Breath counting

Count each exhale from one to ten, then begin again at one. When you lose count, which you will, simply restart. It is the most direct way to train attention.

Body scan

Move your attention slowly from the toes to the crown of the head, noticing sensations without trying to change them. It is easy to do lying down at the end of a practice.

Mantra

Silently repeat a word or phrase with the breath. A traditional choice is so on the inhale and ham on the exhale, often translated as “I am that.” Any calming word works.

Loving-kindness

Rooted in Buddhist practice, this method offers good wishes such as “may you be well, may you be at ease” to yourself, then to loved ones, acquaintances and finally all beings.

Walking meditation

Walk slowly, matching steps to breaths, feeling each foot lift, move and land. It is ideal if sitting still is difficult, or as a break between seated sessions.

Posture for sitting

  • Hips higher than knees: sit on a cushion, block or chair
  • Spine long but not rigid; chin slightly tucked
  • Hands resting on the thighs or in the lap
  • Eyes closed, or softly gazing at the floor ahead
  • Comfort first: it is fine to lie down or lean on a wall

Build the habit

A seven-day starter plan

Five to ten minutes a day, at the same time if you can. Morning, before checking your phone, works well for many people.

  1. Day 1 · Breath awarenessSit and simply notice the natural breath.
    5 min
  2. Day 2 · Three-Part BreathFill and empty the lungs in three stages.
    5 min
  3. Day 3 · Extended exhaleInhale for four, exhale for six.
    5 min
  4. Day 4 · Breath countingCount exhales from one to ten.
    7 min
  5. Day 5 · Alternate-nostril breathingFive rounds, then sit quietly.
    7 min
  6. Day 6 · Body scanLying down, from toes to crown.
    10 min
  7. Day 7 · Your choiceCombine a breathing technique with a meditation method.
    10 min

A note on wellbeing: breathing and meditation practices are not a treatment for anxiety, depression, trauma or any other condition. Some people find that focusing inward can bring up difficult feelings; if that happens, open your eyes, move gently and return to normal breathing.
