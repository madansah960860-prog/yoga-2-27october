Frequently asked questions

Questions we hear most often

Clear answers about starting and sustaining a yoga practice, staying safe on the mat, and how our live sessions, pricing and cancellations work. If your question isn’t here, write to us and we’ll answer it personally.

Starting out

Do I need to be flexible to start yoga?

No. Flexibility is something yoga develops over time, not a requirement for beginning. Every pose in our pose library includes modifications, and props such as blocks and straps bring the floor closer to you. Start where your body is today.

How often should a beginner practice?

Two or three sessions of 20–45 minutes per week is a sustainable start for most people. Consistency matters more than length: three short practices usually teach your body more than one long one. Our beginner’s guide includes a four-week plan.

What should I wear and what equipment do I need?

Wear comfortable clothing that lets you move freely and doesn’t ride up when you fold forward. A non-slip mat is the only essential piece of equipment. Two blocks, a strap and a firm blanket or cushion are helpful extras; household substitutes such as books, a belt and a folded towel work well too.

Which style of yoga is best for beginners?

Hatha, Iyengar-influenced alignment classes, gentle and restorative classes are usually the friendliest starting points because they move at a slower pace and explain each pose. Our yoga styles guide compares ten approaches by pace, intensity and focus.

Can I learn yoga at home from a website?

Yes, many people build a lasting home practice with written and video guidance. Reading instructions carefully, moving slowly and staying within a comfortable range keeps home practice safe. Because no one is watching your alignment, a few live sessions with a teacher can be useful for checking technique and building confidence.

Should I eat before practicing?

Most teachers suggest leaving about two hours after a full meal and 30–60 minutes after a light snack, especially before twists, inversions and deep forward folds. Drink water through the day rather than large amounts right before class.

Safety & wellbeing

Is yoga safe if I have an injury or a health condition?

Yoga can often be adapted, but only your doctor or a qualified healthcare provider can tell you what is appropriate for your situation. Please speak to them before you begin, and let any teacher know about injuries, surgeries or conditions before class. Asana Atlas content and sessions are educational and are not a form of medical treatment or therapy.

Is it normal to feel sore after yoga?

Mild muscle soreness a day or two after a new or more demanding practice is common. Sharp, shooting or joint pain is not; if you feel that during a pose, come out of it slowly. If pain persists, stop practicing and seek medical advice.

Can I practice yoga during pregnancy?

Many people practice throughout pregnancy, but it should only be done with approval from your obstetrician or midwife, and ideally with a teacher trained in prenatal yoga. Deep twists, lying flat on the belly, intense core work, breath retention and hot yoga are commonly avoided. Our general pose instructions are not written for pregnancy.

Will yoga help me lose weight or cure a health problem?

We don’t make those promises. Yoga is a form of physical activity and mindful practice that many people find supports their overall wellbeing, strength and mobility. Results vary between individuals, and yoga is not a replacement for medical care, a balanced diet or treatment prescribed by a professional.

Are inversions such as headstand safe to learn at home?

Full inversions carry a higher risk of neck and shoulder strain and are best learned with in-person or live guidance after you have built strength in foundational poses. People with high blood pressure, glaucoma, neck problems or heart conditions, and those who are pregnant, should avoid them unless cleared by a doctor.

Sessions, pricing & booking

What is the difference between a Professional and an Expert session?

A Professional session ($49, 60 minutes) is a live one-to-one class with a certified teacher, ideal for building a safe, steady practice. An Expert session ($89, 75 minutes) is with a senior teacher and adds a deeper movement intake, advanced asana or breathwork coaching, a session recording and a written two-week plan. Full details are on Sessions & Pricing.

Are there any sign-up fees, subscriptions or hidden charges?

No. You pay the listed price per session, in US dollars, and nothing else. There is no membership, no auto-renewal and no sign-up fee. Any applicable sales tax is shown before you pay.

How do I book and pay?

Send a booking request through our contact page with your preferred session type and times. We reply within one business day to confirm a time and send a secure payment link. You are not charged until you have accepted a confirmed time.

How are sessions delivered?

Sessions are live over video. You’ll receive a private link by email before your session. You need a stable internet connection, a device with a camera you can place a few feet away, and enough floor space to lie down with your arms outstretched.

What is your cancellation and refund policy?

You can cancel or reschedule for free up to 24 hours before your session start time for a full refund. Cancellations within 24 hours are not refunded but can be rescheduled once. If you aren’t satisfied with your first session, tell us within 7 days and we’ll refund it in full. See our Terms & Refunds for the complete policy.

Can I choose my teacher?

Yes. Let us know your preferences, such as focus areas, the time zone you’re in, or a teacher you’ve worked with before, and we’ll match you accordingly. You can request the same teacher for future sessions.
